The hourly rate on the app is not the hourly rate

Gig apps quote a number per job and let you do the division in your head. A job pays 18 dollars, so an hour of the work must be worth about 18 dollars. The quote counts neither the unpaid half of the trip nor the cut the platform keeps. Price both and the real number lands closer to 13 or 14 dollars, which changes which jobs are worth accepting at all. That gap is the whole subject at ExtraHustles: what a gig actually pays per hour once every hidden cost gets counted.

Work one example. Three errand jobs at 18 dollars each is 54 dollars of quoted pay. Each job takes 45 minutes of active time plus another 20 minutes of driving and waiting that no job pays for, so the honest denominator is 3.25 hours instead of 2.25. Against active time alone the quoted rate reads 24 dollars an hour. Apply the platform's 15 percent cut, 8.10 dollars off the batch, and the batch pays 45.90 across 3.25 hours, 14.12 an hour. Subtract tire and brake wear at 7 cents a mile over 24 miles, another 1.68, and the effective rate is 13.61. Same jobs, same day, four numbers the job card never shows.

The habit worth building is pricing the unpaid side before accepting, while the offer is still on the screen. Active time is usually listed or estimable; add a flat 20 minutes per job for deadhead driving and queue waiting, apply the platform's real percentage from your own payout screen, and price mileage at wear cost rather than the tax number, since wear comes out of your pocket eventually. A job that clears 13 or 14 dollars an hour effective is fine work in some towns and a loss in others, and the only way to tell which is to run the division with the whole denominator. Accept the rate you will actually earn.

James Verlander, ExtraHustles
